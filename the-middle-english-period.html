The Middle English Period (1066–1500)

The Middle English Period is a massive transitional era in English literature, initiated by the Norman Conquest of 1066. During this time, the English language transformed dramatically from Old English to a form much closer to modern speech, welcoming a rich blend of French and Latin influences.

To explore this extensive era efficiently, it is traditionally divided into three distinct sub-sections:

1. The Anglo-Norman Period (1066–1340)

Following William the Conqueror's victory, French became the language of the royals and the court. This era introduced chivalric romances, chronicled histories, and religious literature to England.

2. The Age of Chaucer (1340–1400)

Considered the golden age of Middle English literature, dominated by Geoffrey Chaucer, the "Father of English Poetry". This era saw English reclaim its status as a major literary language through masterworks like The Canterbury Tales.

3. The Dark / Barren Period (1400–1485/1500)

A period marked by political unrest, including the War of the Roses. While it saw fewer monumental poetic works compared to Chaucer's time, it laid the ground for modern printing with William Caxton and popular ballads.
